# Natural Capital (concept album bible)

Chosen by Reuben on 2026-11-14 from [`concept-album-options.md`](concept-album-options.md) (Option A). Working document: story, cast, sound and lyric drafts. Everything is fictional. The plot follows the shape of a real public story (investors buying Highland estates for carbon credits and "natural capital", and wind farms paid to switch off when the grid is full), but no real person, company or estate is named or portrayed. Before release, search every character and company name to make sure nobody real shares it.

## The story in one paragraph

Glen Corran is a Highland glen with one village, no affordable houses and a laird who visits for a week a year. The laird sells to **Thistle & Crown Natural Capital**, a London fund. The fund plants conifers on deep peat and sells the "carbon" as credits, books rewilding grants, signs a wind farm that earns more switched off than switched on, and gets a pylon line fast-tracked through the village. Every villain takes a cut. Over one year the trees die on the peat, a reporter starts asking questions and the villains turn on each other. The fund sells the glen on to another fund at a profit, and everyone walks away richer. But the village has organised a community buyout, and in the finale they buy the hill back. The win is real but small: the last track shows the next glen going on sale.

## The witness

**Ewan**, a young crofter who stays in the glen, leaves voicemails for his sister **Morag**, who moved to Glasgow for work. The voicemails are the album's heart and its clock. They are short, plain and spoken, written for Reuben's own speaking voice (or the RVC voice), so no singing or rapping is needed. Over the year Ewan goes from joking, to worried, to angry, to organising. In the last act Morag comes home for the village meeting, and she sings the lead on *We Bought the Hill*. (Names and genders are easy to swap if Reuben prefers.)

## The villains

Each one has a distinct sound so the listener knows who's talking without being told. Each also has a sign-off line in the band's running "due Friday" joke.

| Villain | Who they are | Sound and voice | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Laird** | Old money cashing out (from *Deed to the Glen*) | Sneering raspy rap, Scottish accent, fiddle and pipe drone, distorted 808 | "Rent's due Friday." |
| **Fenella Strachan-Pryce** | Fund partner. Calm, polite, ruthless. Talks about the glen as "the asset" | Close-mic whispered spoken word and cool half-sung lines over minimal glitch trap; English RP | "Settlement's due Friday." |
| **Doc Rory Mair** | Carbon consultant who will certify anything for a fee | Very fast, nerdy, breathless patter rap, chiptune bleeps, clipboard clicks; Edinburgh accent | "Audit's due Friday." |
| **The Grid Man** | Energy trader who profits when the turbines stop | Low, flat, robotic delivery, industrial trap, turbine whoosh as rhythm, Ghost on vocoder hook | "Payment's due Friday." |
| **Tamsin Vale** | Rewilding influencer and brand ambassador | Bright, breathy synth-pop parody that slowly detunes | "Link's in my bio. Due Friday!" |
| **The Spad** | Government special adviser who smooths planning | Smooth crooner over swing horns and 808s, spoken asides | "Consultation closes Friday." |
| **Cousin Gav** | Local lad hired as ghillie, then security. The only villain with a conscience | Acoustic bluegrass ballad, cracked tired voice | (none; he doesn't say it, which is the point) |

## Sound of the album

- **Core:** the band's dark Celtic country trap, with banjo, fiddle, distorted 808 and glitches.
- **Sound follows the plot.** Act 1 is glossy and confident. Act 2 is fun and busy, with each villain in their own genre. Act 3 is broken up: beats stutter and glitches eat the hooks. Act 4 starts dark and heavy and then breaks out into acoustic folk-punk.
- **Motifs that come back:**
  1. **The muzak.** Estate-agent hold music in track 1, licensed into the fund's jingle in track 4, slowed and broken in track 14, and back unchanged in track 16 for the next glen.
  2. **The pipes.** A lone lament is cut off in track 1. A chopped pipe drone becomes the fund's jingle in track 4. A single slow air plays under Gav's last chorus. The full pipes finally play properly and loud in *We Bought the Hill*.
  3. **The phone line.** Every voicemail has the same beep and the same line hiss, and each one gets a little more wind behind it as the season turns.
  4. **"Due Friday."** Every villain gets one. In the finale the village answers it.
- **Bagpipe captions.** Write "brief Highland bagpipe drone in the intro" or "solo bagpipe lament, sparse", never plain "bagpipes", or they flood the track. Pipe songs work best in B-flat or A.

## Track by track (about 47 minutes)

### Act 1: Spring, the sale

**1. Viewing by Appointment Only** (0:50, skit). A lone pipe lament on a hillside is cut off by estate-agent hold music. An estate agent gives a cheerful video tour: "forty thousand acres, minimal community, excellent sporting rights, tremendous natural capital." The muzak glitches out. *Beat:* the glen is for sale and nobody local was asked.

**2. Deed to the Glen** (3:40, existing). The Laird's farewell boast. Keep the current take (repaint 7001, pedalboard master). *Beat:* we meet the old system before the new one replaces it. In the album's spoken outro, swap the last line for "Rent's due Friday... to someone else now."

**3. Voicemail 1: "They've Sold It"** (0:40, spoken). Ewan's first voicemail. He's half amused: the laird has sold up, nobody knows who to. *Beat:* sets up the witness, his sister and the clock (it's April).

**4. Natural Capital** (5:30, the scheme song). Fenella's launch-party pitch to investors turns into an ensemble anthem: each villain gets a short guest bar to introduce themselves. Sparse glitch trap that builds; the pipe drone is chopped into the fund's jingle. *Beat:* the whole scheme is explained in plain words, so the rest of the album can just play.

### Act 2: Summer, the boom

**5. Certified** (2:10). Doc Rory raps his audit at double speed: he'll certify anything, including trees on peat, deer as "biodiversity units" and a car park as wetland. Chiptune and clipboard clicks. *Beat:* the credits are worth whatever he says they're worth.

**6. Paid to Stop** (4:20). The Grid Man explains why he loves calm nights and full cables: the turbines are paid to stop. Industrial trap with a turbine whoosh as the hi-hat. *Beat:* the money now flows whether the wind blows or not.

**7. Beaver Season** (2:45). Tamsin's brand-launch video for the "Glen Corran Experience": glamping pods at £400 a night, beavers, cold-water wellness. Bright synth-pop that slowly goes out of tune. *Beat:* the glen is now a product, and the locals are in the way of the view.

**8. Voicemail 2: "No Houses"** (0:55, spoken over a lone fiddle). Ewan's flat lease isn't renewed: the cottage is becoming a glamping hub. The school roll is down to nine. *Beat:* the cost lands on real people. It's July.

**9. Fast Track** (3:05). The Spad croons about getting the pylon line and the planning through: "the consultation was very well attended, by me." Swing horns over 808s. *Beat:* the state is in on it.

**10. Pylons** (1:30, instrumental). Drone, electrical hum and banjo plucks as the line goes up. *Beat:* a breath before the turn, and the landscape physically changes.

### Act 3: Autumn, the cracks

**11. Gav's Song** (4:00). Cousin Gav, paid to keep walkers off the hill he grew up on, sings an acoustic bluegrass ballad about what he's become. A single slow pipe air under the last chorus. *Beat:* the turn. One villain sees it.

**12. Dead Sitka** (3:15). A reporter's call. The trees are dying on the peat. Doc Rory and the Grid Man panic in a paranoid half-time duet, and glitches eat their hooks. *Beat:* the scheme starts to wobble.

**13. Voicemail 3: "There's a Meeting"** (0:35, spoken, village-hall noise behind). Gav came to Ewan with the paperwork. The village is meeting about a community buyout. "Come home, Morag." *Beat:* the witness becomes an organiser. It's October.

### Act 4: Winter, the reckoning

**14. Exit Strategy** (6:10). Fenella's centrepiece. As the reporter's story breaks, she calmly sells the glen on to another fund at a profit, while the other villains rage and blame each other on a conference call. Dark epic with a nu-metal chorus. The muzak returns slowed and broken. *Beat:* the villains don't lose, they leave.

**15. We Bought the Hill** (3:30, existing). Morag comes home and leads the village. Celtic folk-punk, full pipes at last. *Beat:* the community buyout wins. (A spoken line from Ewan at the very start ties it in: "She came home.")

**16. Under New Ownership** (0:45, hidden coda). The hold music again, untouched. A different estate agent is giving a video tour of the next glen: "...tremendous natural capital." Cut. *Beat:* the system goes on.

## Next steps

1. Reuben reads the beats and the three drafts; change anything that doesn't feel right.
2. Make *Natural Capital* and *Voicemail 1* on the laptop as the proof of concept (one big ensemble song plus one spoken piece, to test the two halves of the album together). The Laird's outro line in *Deed to the Glen* can be repainted later. One take probably won't get five distinct voices right, so plan on a main take (Fenella plus the chorus) and then repaint each guest bar in verse 2 with its own caption, the same way we patched *Deed to the Glen*.
3. Then draft Act 2 (*Certified*, *Paid to Stop*, *Beaver Season*, *Voicemail 2*, *Fast Track*).
