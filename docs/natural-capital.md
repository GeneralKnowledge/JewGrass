# Natural Capital (concept album bible, v2)

Chosen by Reuben on 2026-11-14 from [`concept-album-options.md`](concept-album-options.md) (Option A), then widened the same night: 16 tracks need a bigger story, about **billions, corruption and power**, told as hard political storytelling rap that teaches history and follows the money, from a **British, Scottish point of view**. The band's own voice and sound only; no imitation of any artist.

Everything is fictional. The plot follows the shape of real public stories (listed under *Real ground* below), but every person, company, trust and estate is invented. No real person is named, portrayed or accused. Before release, search every character and company name to make sure nobody real shares it.

The title now means two things: the "natural capital" the fund says it's buying in the glen, and **the Capital**, London, where the money actually goes.

## The story in one paragraph

Glen Corran is sold by its absentee laird to a London fund that wants carbon credits. Ewan, a young crofter, tries to find out who now owns his home. The buyer turns out to be a **Scottish limited partnership** registered at a Glasgow letterbox shared with four hundred other companies. Ewan and his sister Morag, a junior reporter in Glasgow, follow the money from that letterbox, and it leads through a whole **consortium** of people who all invested in the glen. There's an oil boss and a water boss living off bills, a contractor who made a fortune selling useless kit to the state in an emergency, a donor turned lord who fixes the planning, a newspaper owner who smears the village, and finally an offshore trust on a sunny island where none of it is ever taxed. Every hop is a song. Morag gets the story published. Nobody goes to jail; the consortium sells the glen on at a profit and moves to the next one. But the village uses the scandal to win a community buyout, and buys its hill back.

**The thesis:** this isn't new. The album opens and peaks with the band telling the long version: Scotland bought and sold, from a failed colonial gamble and the Union, through the Clearances and the oil money, to carbon credits and letterbox companies. Same glen, same leaving, better branding.

## Real ground (what the satire stands on)

Used for shape and for the history track only. All public, all about systems rather than named individuals.

- **Green lairds and carbon credits.** Investors buying Highland estates for "natural capital" ([AP via ADN, 2025](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2025/02/18/green-lairds-are-buying-vast-estates-in-scotland-for-carbon-credits/), [Community Land Scotland](https://www.communitylandscotland.org.uk/scotlands-new-mega-lairds-now-controlling-huge-areas-of-scotland/)), and wind farms paid to switch off when the grid is full ([The Ferret](https://www.theferret.scot/why-are-scottish-wind-farms-paid-billions-to-switch-off/)).
- **Scottish limited partnerships.** A Scottish company form widely reported as used to move laundered billions, which led to reform and new rules on who owns them ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/crackdown-on-abuse-of-uk-businesses-for-foreign-money-laundering), [RUSI](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/one-step-time-limited-reform-uks-limited-partnerships)).
- **Emergency-contract waste.** The government's Covid counter-fraud commissioner estimated about £10.9bn lost to fraud and error in pandemic schemes, much of it beyond recovery ([gov.uk response](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/final-report-of-the-covid-counter-fraud-commissioner/government-response-to-the-final-report-of-the-covid-counter-fraud-commissioner), [Nation.Cymru](https://nation.cymru/news/covid-19-fraud-cost-taxpayers-10-9bn-but-much-beyond-recovery-report-finds)).
- **Britain's offshore network.** UK overseas territories and Crown dependencies ranked among the most damaging tax havens in the world ([The Ferret](https://www.theferret.scot/uk-overseas-territories-world-worst-tax-havens/), [Tax Justice Network](https://taxjustice.net/press/correction-countries-are-losing-1-7-more-to-tax-havens-than-we-reported/)).
- **Water and energy.** Debt-loaded water companies, record energy profits while bills rose (already in *Swim at Your Own Risk* and *Cold Hame*).
- **History (for *Parcel of Rogues* and *Bought and Sold*).** The Darien scheme (1690s), the Union of 1707 and Burns's *Such a Parcel of Rogues in a Nation* (public domain, 1791), the Highland Clearances, and the 1974 government report on the value of North Sea oil to Scotland that was kept secret for about thirty years.

## The witnesses

- **Ewan** stays in the glen. His voicemails to Morag are the album's clock and conscience. They're short, plain and spoken, written for Reuben's own speaking voice (or the RVC voice), so no singing or rapping is needed. Over the year he goes from joking, to digging, to organising.
- **Morag**, his sister, is a junior reporter in Glasgow who takes the story on. She's heard in the background of the voicemails, then sings lead on *We Bought the Hill* when she comes home.
- **The band itself** steps out of the story twice (the prologue and *Bought and Sold*) to speak directly: the history lesson, in its own angry voice.

(Names and genders are easy to swap if Reuben prefers.)

The money-trail motif: in each villain's song, one line says where "the pound" goes next (Ewan's rent pound, followed hop by hop from the glen to the island).

## Sound of the album

- **Core:** the band's dark Celtic country trap, with banjo, fiddle, distorted 808, glitches and nu-metal guitars on the biggest choruses.
- **Sound follows the money.** Act 1 (the glen) is local and acoustic-leaning. Act 2 (the Capital) is glossy, busy and expensive, with each villain in their own genre. Act 3 (offshore) is smooth and empty, then the band breaks it open with *Bought and Sold*. Act 4 starts dark and heavy and ends in acoustic folk-punk.
- **Motifs that come back:**
  1. **The hold music.** Estate-agent muzak in track 2, licensed into the fund's jingle in track 5, the lobby music at the island in track 12, slowed and broken in track 15, back unchanged at the end for the next glen.
  2. **The pipes.** The prologue opens on a pipe lament. A chopped pipe drone becomes the fund's jingle. A single slow air plays under Gav's last chorus. The full pipes finally play properly and loud in *We Bought the Hill*.
  3. **The phone line.** Every voicemail has the same beep and hiss.
  4. **"Due Friday."** Every villain has one. In the finale the village answers it.
  5. **"Bought and sold."** The Burns line from the prologue comes back in the bridge of *Bought and Sold* and as the gang chant in *Exit Strategy*.
- **Bagpipe captions.** Write "brief Highland bagpipe drone in the intro" or "solo bagpipe lament, sparse", never plain "bagpipes". Pipe songs work best in B-flat or A.

## Track by track (16 tracks, about 54 minutes)

### Prologue

**1. Parcel of Rogues** (1:20). The band, spoken. A pipe lament and wind; a voice reads Burns's line about being bought and sold, then the history in four quick snapshots (Darien, 1707, the Clearances, the oil) as the pipes are swallowed by a stock-ticker glitch. *Beat:* sets the thesis: this has happened before.

### Act 1: The Glen (spring)

**2. Viewing by Appointment Only** (0:50, skit). The estate agent's video tour: "forty thousand acres, minimal community, tremendous natural capital." *Beat:* the glen is for sale and nobody local was asked.

**3. Deed to the Glen** (3:40, existing). The Laird's farewell boast. In the album outro: "Rent's due Friday... to someone else now."

**4. Voicemail: "They've Sold It"** (0:40). Ewan: the laird has sold up, and nobody knows who to. *Beat:* the question that starts the whole investigation.

**5. Natural Capital** (5:30). Fenella's launch party, with guest bars from Doc Rory, the Grid Man and Tamsin the rewilding influencer. *Beat:* the local scheme explained, and a hint that the money behind it is far bigger than the glen.

### Act 2: The Capital (summer)

**6. Voicemail: "Four Hundred Companies"** (0:50). Ewan has looked up the buyer: a partnership registered at a Glasgow letterbox above a vape shop, shared with four hundred other companies. Morag is starting to listen. *Beat:* the money trail begins.

**7. Brass Plate** (2:40). Malky the formation agent: "I can make you a company before your tea's gone cold." He doesn't ask who the partners are, and he's very proud of it. *Beat:* how anonymous money hides in plain sight, in Scotland.

**8. Cold Hame** (3:30, existing). One of the partners: the oil boss. *Beat:* the first big investor, energy bills into land.

**9. Fast Lane** (4:10). Gideon the contractor, who got waved through a fast lane in a national emergency and sold the state gowns that never came and masks that never fitted, then put the profit into "nature". *Beat:* the second investor, public money straight out the door.

**10. Swim at Your Own Risk** (4:00, existing). The water boss, the third investor. *Beat:* the pattern is clear: everyone in the fund got rich from public services.

**11. Ermine and Ink** (3:20). Lord Pellingham, a donor given a seat in the Lords, fixes the planning, and Sir Desmond's papers call the villagers "NIMBY crofters blocking Britain's green future." *Beat:* politics and the press protect the deal. Morag's first article gets spiked.

### Act 3: Offshore (autumn)

**12. Sunshine Trust** (3:50). It opens with a short voicemail from Ewan: "It goes to an island." Then Clarissa, the trust manager, explains silkily that the fund's profits are owned by a trust, owned by a foundation, owned by nobody, on a sunny British island where none of it is taxed. *Beat:* the end of the money trail. It was British all along.

**13. Bought and Sold** (5:00). The band steps out of the story and speaks directly: the long history lesson from Darien to the letterbox, angry and precise. The album's centrepiece and its thesis. *Beat:* the listener sees the whole system, not just the villains.

**14. Gav's Song** (4:00). Cousin Gav, paid to keep walkers off his own hill, leaks the paperwork that ties every investor together. A single slow pipe air under the last chorus. *Beat:* the turn. Morag has her proof.

### Act 4: The reckoning (winter)

**15. Exit Strategy** (6:10). Morag's story is published. The consortium holds an emergency conference call: blame, panic, then calm, as Fenella sells the glen on to another fund at a profit. Dark epic, nu-metal chorus, the gang chant "bought and sold". *Beat:* the villains don't lose, they leave.

**16. We Bought the Hill** (4:20, existing plus coda). Ewan: "She came home." Morag leads the village to a community buyout, with the full pipes at last. Then, hidden at the end, the hold music returns untouched as a new estate agent tours the next glen. *Beat:* a real but small win, and the system goes on.

## What changed from v1

- The story now follows the money from the glen to Glasgow, London and offshore, instead of staying in one glen.
- The consortium grew from 7 to 10 villains, and the existing songs now sit inside the story: *Deed to the Glen* (the seller), *Cold Hame* and *Swim at Your Own Risk* (two of the investors) and *We Bought the Hill* (the finale).
- New: *Parcel of Rogues*, *Brass Plate*, *Fast Lane*, *Ermine and Ink*, *Sunshine Trust* and *Bought and Sold*. Cut: *Certified*, *Paid to Stop*, *Beaver Season*, *Fast Track* and *Pylons* (their characters survive as guests on *Natural Capital*), *Dead Sitka*, and two voicemails.
- Morag is now a reporter, so the story has a reason to end in a scandal.
