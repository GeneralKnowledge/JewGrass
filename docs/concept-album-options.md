# Concept album: three options for one shared villain scheme

Written 2026-11-14. Research and writing only, no audio yet.

The brief: a future experimental concept album that tells one story from start to finish, the way a narrative album follows one character through a whole arc. The villains should be more varied than the current run (landlord, laird, water boss) and should **work together on one scheme**, ideally drawn from a real recent UK story. Everything here is original: our own characters, plot and sound, with no imitation of any artist.

## How we keep it legal (read this first)

- **Real events, fictional people.** Each plot borrows the *shape* of a real public story (what happened, the money flows, the reported facts), but every character is invented or a composite. No real person, company, estate, ship or fund is named in lyrics, titles, artwork or captions.
- **Invent the names of places and companies too** where the real one would point at someone (e.g. "Glen Corran Estate", "Albion Waters", "MV Glen Bonnie").
- **Satirise the system, not a person.** The villains boast about things that were publicly reported as legal-but-grim (paying to switch turbines off, debt-funded dividends, cost overruns). We never have a character who is recognisably a real person confess to a crime.
- A short sleeve note like "All characters are fictional. Any resemblance to real persons is coincidental" goes on the release.

## How a story album works (structure, not sound)

What we borrow is only the storytelling toolkit, which is common to many concept albums and musicals:

1. **One clock.** The tracks run in story order, so the listener feels time pass (a year, a season, a parliamentary term).
2. **A plain-speaking witness.** Someone who is not a villain appears in short interludes between the big songs, so the story has a point of view and a heart. This is a great fit for Reuben's own voice: **spoken word, no singing or rapping needed**, which suits the RVC voice work in `docs/voice/`.
3. **Sound follows plot.** The style changes when the story turns: swagger early, paranoia in the middle, collapse or reckoning at the end.
4. **Recurring motifs.** A hook, a phrase or an instrument comes back changed (our running "Rent's due Friday" joke is already one).
5. **Varied lengths.** 30-second skits and voicemails next to 6-minute centrepieces, so the album breathes.

## Option A (recommended): *Natural Capital*

**The scheme.** A London-registered fund, **Thistle & Crown Natural Capital**, wants to "green" a whole Highland glen. It buys **Glen Corran Estate** from the old laird, claims carbon credits for planting conifers on peat, books grants for "rewilding", signs a wind farm that gets paid more to *switch off* than to run, and routes a pylon line through the village. On paper it is the greenest thing in Scotland. The villagers can't get a house, a job or onto their own hill. In the end they try a community buyout.

**The villains (all fictional, deliberately different).**

| Character | Archetype | Voice and style idea |
|---|---|---|
| **The Old Laird** (from *Deed to the Glen*) | Faded aristocrat cashing out | Wildcard, drunk drawl over slow country trap |
| **Fenella Strachan-Pryce, Fund Partner** | Polished City financier, all spreadsheets | Cold spoken word over minimal glitch, ASMR-close mic |
| **"Doc" Rory Mair, Carbon Consultant** | Greenwash auditor who certifies anything | Fast nerdy patter rap, chiptune bleeps |
| **The Grid Man** | Energy trader who profits when the turbines stop | Industrial techno trap, robotic Ghost hook |
| **Tamsin, the Rewilding Influencer** | Brand ambassador, beavers and "wellness" | Bright synth-pop parody that curdles |
| **The Special Adviser** | Government fixer who smooths the planning | Smooth crooner swing, spoken asides |
| **Cousin Gav, Ghillie turned Security** | Local lad bought off, the most human villain | Bluegrass ballad, guilty and torn |

**The witness.** **Morag**, a young crofter who keeps leaving voicemails to her brother who moved to Glasgow. These are Reuben's spoken interludes. She is also the lead voice of *We Bought the Hill*, which becomes the finale.

**Story arc (one year, seasons as acts).**
- *Spring, the sale.* The laird sells, the fund arrives with a launch party.
- *Summer, the boom.* Everyone gets their cut; the scheme looks unstoppable.
- *Autumn, the cracks.* The trees die on the peat, a journalist calls, the consultant's numbers wobble, the villains start blaming each other.
- *Winter, the reckoning.* The fund sells the glen on to another fund at a profit; the village launches its buyout and wins back the hill. (Bittersweet: the villains all walk away richer.)

## Option B: *Special Administration*

**The scheme.** **Albion Waters**, a fictional utility, is drowning in debt after years of dividends and spills. As collapse nears, a ring of players work together to make sure the public pays and they don't: the boss from *Swim at Your Own Risk*, a hedge-fund creditor who wants the company cheap, a regulator chief who keeps "consulting", a lobbyist, a crisis-PR firm, and a bonus-collecting interim CEO.

**Cast.** The Water Boss (existing), **Clive the Creditor** (posh bond trader), **The Regulator** (beige, terrified, bureaucratic spoken word), **Lobbyist Lou** (smarmy Westminster patter), **Crisis PR** (two-voice slick duet), **The Interim CEO** (motivational-speaker rap). Witness: a **wild swimmer and sewage campaigner** recording water-sample voice notes.

**Arc.** A hot summer of spills and hosepipe bans, a debt deadline, the rescue-or-nationalise fight, and a final deal where the bills go up and the same people stay in charge. The witness's last voice note is the sample results: still dirty.

**Track list (about 40 minutes).** 1 Voice Note: Sample 1 (0:30) · 2 Swim at Your Own Risk (4:00, existing) · 3 Leverage (3:20, trap with stock-ticker glitches) · 4 Consultation Period (5:00, deliberately boring drone that never resolves, the joke is the length) · 5 Voice Note: Sample 2 (0:40) · 6 Messaging (2:30, PR duet, glossy R&B parody) · 7 Corridors (3:00, lobbyist, smooth jazz-trap) · 8 Hosepipe Ban (1:50, punk) · 9 Golden Share (4:40, Clive, orchestral trap) · 10 Interim (2:45, motivational rap) · 11 Bills Up (3:30, gang chant, everyone) · 12 Voice Note: Final Results (1:10, spoken, unaccompanied).

**Trade-off.** Tight and funny, and the first song exists, but it is the least Scottish and leans on one very recognisable company, so names and details need extra care.

## Option C: *Six Years Late*

**The scheme.** An island needs one new ferry. The fictional **MV Glen Bonnie** is ordered on a tight budget and a happy announcement, then bounces between a shipyard boss, a procurement official, a minister desperate for a photo op, a design consultant who keeps redesigning, and a cable supplier. Every one of them profits from the delay. The ship is launched with **painted-on windows** for the cameras. Islanders wait six years.

**Cast.** **The Minister** (campaign-jingle pop), **The Yard Gaffer** (gruff Clyde shipyard work-song rap), **The Procurement Officer** (deadpan spoken spreadsheet), **The Consultant** (redesign after redesign, each verse in a new genre), **The Blame Committee** (all villains as a barbershop-style vocal group), and the **ship itself**, voiced by Ghost through heavy vocoder. Witness: an **island shopkeeper** whose shop depends on the ferry, in phone-in radio clips.

**Arc.** Told one year per act, from launch-day champagne to the first sailing. Each year the same chorus returns more exhausted and glitched, and the budget number in the lyrics keeps climbing.

**Track list (about 44 minutes).** 1 Launch Day (2:30, brass band into trap) · 2 Painted Windows (3:40, the hit single) · 3 Phone-In: Year One (0:45) · 4 Variation Order (4:10, the Consultant, changes genre every 40 seconds) · 5 Cables (2:20, industrial) · 6 Phone-In: Year Three (0:50) · 7 Not My Department (3:30, the Blame Committee, barbershop meets trap) · 8 Sea Trials (6:00, the ship's ballad, vocoder, slow) · 9 Phone-In: Year Five (0:40) · 10 Ribbon (2:50, the Minister, triumphant and hollow) · 11 Maiden Voyage (4:30, full band, the ship finally sails, the shop has already closed) · 12 Timetable (1:15, a calm announcement listing cancellations, fades out).

**Trade-off.** The funniest premise and the most musically adventurous (a genre-hopping track, a singing ship), but the real story involves identifiable officials, so it needs the most careful handling and is the furthest from our current songs.

## Mixing options

These are not exclusive. A's fund could own B's water company, and the Grid Man could reappear on a later album. A good long-term shape is a **trilogy of the same "consortium"** wrecking a different public good on each record: land, then water, then transport.
