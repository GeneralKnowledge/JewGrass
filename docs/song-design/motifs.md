# Motif library

Five pieces of real musical material that the album reuses, transposes and transforms. The notes are in [`motifs.yaml`](motifs.yaml); `tools/design/motif_audition.py` renders a rough audition of each so we can judge the tunes by ear before they go anywhere near a song.

All five are original. They are deliberately short (one bar or two), because short ideas survive transformation and are easy for a listener to spot.

## The five themes

| Motif | Notes (in its home key) | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | A F# E D B, falling, Scotch snap on the first two notes, over an A drone | The land being taken. Always played on pipes or something that sounds old. |
| **Village** | B D E F# A, F# E D D, in 6/8 | The ownership phrase played backwards and carried on. The village answers the theft by reversing its tune. |
| **Money** | C G Db C, four quick notes | A cash register with a bad conscience. Electronic, small, catchy. |
| **Hold music** | A Bb C A, G A F, over F Dm Bb C | Cheerful lounge tune. Harmless at first, which is the joke. |
| **Corruption** | Cm, Db, Cm, B diminished, all over a low C | A progression that never settles. Used under the moments where the deal is done. |

Why these keys: Highland pipes play one scale (A mixolydian, which is the notes of D major). Any song where the pipes play has to sit in D major, B minor or A, or the pipes clash. Ownership and village are written in that scale so the pipes can always carry them. Money and corruption are in C minor, as far from the pipes as possible: they are the sound of the City, not the glen.

## How each motif develops across the album

The point is that a motif changes meaning, not that it reappears.

### Ownership (pipes)
| Track | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 1 Parcel of Rogues | Slow solo lament, full phrase, swallowed by glitch at the end |
| 5 Natural Capital | Chopped into a two-note jingle for the fund's launch (the land sold back as branding) |
| 13 Bought and Sold | Opens the song as a lament; the final note of the album's centrepiece is the pipe phrase alone, left hanging on A |
| 14 Gav's Song | Turned upside down: the pipe plays it rising in the final chorus (this becomes the village theme) |
| 16 We Bought the Hill | Full pipes play ownership then village back to back, loud |

### Village
| Track | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 3 Deed to the Glen | Not heard. The village has no voice yet |
| 14 Gav's Song | First appearance: the fiddle hints at its first three notes in chorus 2, the pipe plays it fully in the last chorus |
| 16 We Bought the Hill | The whole village sings it as the chorus melody |

### Money
| Track | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 5 Natural Capital | Glassy synth, clean, beautiful |
| 7 Brass Plate | Harpsichord, played as a comic flourish every time a company is made |
| 9 Fast Lane | Revving 808 slide on the same four notes |
| 12 Sunshine Trust | Steel pan, slow and pretty, so it sounds like a holiday |
| 15 Exit Strategy | Distorted, slowed down, stuck in a loop |

### Hold music
| Track | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 2 Viewing by Appointment Only | A harmless little piano tune while the estate agent talks |
| 5 Natural Capital | Licensed: a bright corporate jingle |
| 12 Sunshine Trust | The lobby music on the island, slowed and sun-soaked |
| 15 Exit Strategy | Slowed, broken, sinister under the conference call |
| 16 We Bought the Hill | Hidden at the very end, back exactly as it was in track 2, for the next glen |

### Corruption
| Track | Treatment |
|---|---|
| 7 Brass Plate | Under verse 3, the moment Malky finds out he owns the glen |
| 11 Ermine and Ink | String quartet, under the planning decision |
| 15 Exit Strategy | Church organ, the whole progression, very slow |

## The lyric motifs evolve too

### "Due Friday"
Every villain still has one, but the meaning moves from joke to threat to answer. Gav, the only non-villain, gets one too, and it is the one that hurts.

| Stage | Tracks | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Joke | 3 Deed to the Glen, 5 Natural Capital | A throwaway sign-off. The listener laughs. |
| Joke with a sting | 7 Brass Plate | Folded into the last chorus, and the filing turns out to be his own |
| Routine | 8 to 12 | Each villain's version gets shorter and colder; Sunshine Trust's "Nothing's due. Ever." is the end of the joke |
| History | 13 Bought and Sold | "They've had a Friday every week for three hundred years, and every week it's us that pays." No longer funny |
| Human cost | 14 Gav's Song | Friday is payday: the wage that keeps him quiet. In the last chorus the Friday money is sitting there and he will not touch it |
| Threat | 15 Exit Strategy | Said quietly, once, as a deadline for the village |
| Answer | 16 We Bought the Hill | The village answers it ("Paid in full. Friday.", or similar, to be written with that song) |

### "Bought and sold"
Prologue reads the Burns line, *Bought and Sold* turns it into the chorus, *Exit Strategy* turns it into a chant the villains sneer, and the finale turns it round ("Not for sale").

### The money trail
One line per villain says where the pound goes next. Brass Plate: "The pound that paid the laird's rent comes through my door at two, and by quarter past it's sailing to an island." Gav's Song: "Frae an island I couldnae point to."
