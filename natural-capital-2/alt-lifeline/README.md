# Lifeline (Natural Capital, part 2) — UNUSED DRAFT

> Superseded on 2026-11-15: Reuben redirected part 2 to the "files" album (see ../README.md). Kept here because we never delete old versions. Only tracks 1-3 were written.

The second album by the same band that made *Natural Capital*, asked for by Reuben on 2026-11-15 ("I want another album from the same band"). It uses the two concepts that weren't picked last time, *Special Administration* (the water company rescue) and *Six Years Late* (the ferry fiasco), and joins them with the same family and the same villain.

Same rules as part 1: the plot follows the shape of real public stories, but every person, company, ship and place is invented. No real person is named, portrayed or accused. Before release, search every character, company and ship name to make sure nobody real shares it. The band's own voice and sound only; no imitation of any artist.

**What's different this time:** much more singing. Part 1 ended up mostly rap. Here 8 of the 12 songs are sung-led (ballads, a lullaby, a punk shout-along, a country-pop single and a folk-punk finale), and the rap goes to the villains, where it does a job.

## The story in one paragraph

A year after the village bought its hill back, Glen Corran owns its land but not its lifelines. Morag, the reporter who broke the *Natural Capital* story, now works on a national paper in London, where she's been put on the water beat: **Albion Waters**, the company run by the Water Boss from *Swim at Your Own Risk*, is drowning in debt and pumping sewage into the rivers. Back home, Ewan chairs the community trust and waits for a ferry. The glen sits at the end of a peninsula, ninety miles round by road and ten minutes by boat, and the old boat keeps breaking down. A new one, the **MV Glen Bonnie**, is ordered with champagne and a minister's photo op. It's launched with its windows painted on for the cameras, and it takes six years. Morag follows the money in London and finds the same name in both stories: **Fenella Strachan-Pryce**, the fund manager from part 1, has bought Albion's debt for pennies so she wins whether it's rescued or not, and her second fund charters the stopgap ferry to the government at a fortune a day. Albion goes into special administration; the public pays, the bills go up and the same people stay in charge. Nobody resigns over the ferry either. But in the last song the Glen Bonnie finally sails, and Morag is on it, coming home for good.

**The thesis:** private or public, it doesn't matter who owns the line if nobody answers for it when it breaks. Part 1 followed the money out of Scotland. Part 2 shows that the lifelines break at home too: a London-owned water company and a ferry built by our own lot both leave the same people stranded.

## Real ground (what the satire stands on)

All public stories about systems, used for shape only:

- **Ferries.** Two Scottish lifeline ferries ordered in 2015 came in years late and several times over budget; one was launched in 2017 with windows painted on for the ceremony. Islanders lived with cancellations while old ships broke down and stopgap vessels were chartered or bought at high cost.
- **Water.** English water companies were privatised in 1989, loaded with debt while paying dividends, fined for sewage spills, and one of the largest came close to the government's **special administration** regime. Scottish Water stayed public, which is why the album's water company is English-owned and the glen's problem is the ferry.
- **Bond vultures.** Distressed-debt funds buy the bonds of failing companies cheaply and profit in a rescue or a collapse.

## The witnesses

- **Ewan** stays in the glen and chairs the community trust. His voice notes (Reuben's own speaking voice, or the RVC voice) are the album's clock: year one, year three, year five, year six. Over the album he goes from patient, to furious, to tired, to laughing.
- **Morag** sings this time. She leads *Hosepipe Ban*, the *Swim* reprise and the finale.
- **The band** opens the album in its own voice with *Lifeline*.

**The running joke.** Part 1's villains all signed off "due Friday". In part 2 every villain signs off with a cancellation notice, each with their own excuse. The finale answers it: *"Glen Bonnie. Sailing. On time."*

All excuses are sung inside the last section, never spoken after the music: every song starts straight in and ends sung.

## Sound of the album

- **Core:** the band's dark Celtic country trap (banjo, fiddle, distorted 808, glitch) with nu-metal guitars on the biggest choruses, but with more room for voices: acoustic guitar, piano and strings carry the sung songs.
- **Water songs** (London) are glossy, cold and expensive: dark trap, synth bass, whispered vocals.
- **Ferry songs** (home) are warm, live and acoustic-leaning: folk, country, punk, with the 808 underneath.
- **The pipes:** held back almost completely. A brief drone opens *Lifeline*; the full pipes play only in the last chorus of *Maiden Voyage*.
- **The sea:** the sung ferry songs are in D major or B minor, the keys the pipes use, so the finale can bring them in.
- **Caption rules (all acts):** no crackle, hiss, static, vinyl, gramophone or typewriter; nothing circus-like (tuba, pizzicato, woodblock, marching snare, comic, theatrical); always "warm cohesive blended mix", never "clean polished mix"; few named solo instruments; start straight in; end sung. `build.py` refuses captions that break these.

### Track by track

1. **Lifeline.** The band sings about the lines a place hangs by: a water pipe, a ferry rope, a phone line, and who holds the other end. *Beat:* the question of the album.
2. **Voice Note: Year One.** Ewan: the hill's ours, the shop's busy, the old ferry's broken again, and Morag's off to London.
3. **Hosepipe Ban.** A heatwave in London. Morag, new on the water beat, is banned from watering a window box in a country made of rain while Albion pumps sewage into the river. *Beat:* she's on the story.
4. **Leverage.** Fenella is back. She's bought Albion's debt at forty pence in the pound: if it's rescued she's paid, if it's broken up she's paid first. *Beat:* the same name as last time.
5. **Minded To.** The Regulator sings the public to sleep: "we are minded to consult". *Beat:* nobody is coming to stop it.
6. **Voice Note: The Boat's Off.** Ewan: the old ferry's off for a month, the road round is ninety miles, but there's a new boat ordered and a minister's coming.
7. **Painted Windows.** The launch day of the MV Glen Bonnie: flags, champagne, a big chorus, and windows painted on for the cameras. The catchiest song on the record and the angriest joke. *Beat:* promises.
8. **Variation Order.** The Yard Gaffer explains why it's late: every change is a variation, every variation is a fee. *Beat:* the delay pays.
9. **Day Rate.** Hector the charter broker rents the government a thirty-year-old stopgap ferry at a fortune a day. Morag finds out whose fund owns it. *Beat:* the two stories join.
10. **Sea Trials.** The ship herself sings, stuck at the quay for years, rusting in her own paint. *Beat:* the cost, felt.
11. **Voice Note: Year Five.** Ewan: the shop's closing, Mrs Grant's moving to her daughter's, and the Glen Bonnie's had another launch.
12. **Special Administration.** Albion Waters collapses. The Interim CEO pitches the rescue; the crisis PR team "manages the narrative"; Fenella cashes out; the bills go up. *Beat:* the water story ends the way part 1 did: the villains leave richer.
13. **Not My Department.** Every villain from both stories blames someone else, in harmony. *Beat:* nobody answers for anything.
14. **Swim at Your Own Risk (Reprise).** Morag at the river, slow and quiet, singing the old villain's hook back at him from the other side. *Beat:* she's done with London.
15. **Voice Note: She's on the Boat.** Ewan, laughing: the Glen Bonnie's sailing at last, and Morag's on it.
16. **Maiden Voyage.** Six years late, the ship sails and the village meets her at the pier. The shop reopens as a co-op. Full pipes, at last. *Beat:* a small, real win: "Glen Bonnie. Sailing. On time."
